# Paychecks

> **Estimates, not tax advice.** Budget Buddy helps you keep your pay stubs organized and spot things worth a second look. Check your W-2 and the IRS instructions, tax software, or a tax professional before you file.

## What to enter

Copy each line from your pay stub.

| What to enter | Where to find it on your pay stub |
| --- | --- |
| Pay date | The date you were paid (the "check date") |
| Employer | Who paid you |
| Gross pay | Total pay before anything is taken out |
| Federal tax | Federal income tax withheld |
| State tax | State income tax withheld |
| Social Security | Social Security tax (often "OASDI" on the stub) |
| Medicare | Medicare tax |
| 401(k)/403(b) before tax | Traditional 401(k), 403(b) or 457 (taken before taxes) |
| HSA | HSA contributions taken from your pay (taken before taxes) |
| Health insurance premiums | Health, dental, vision premiums, and FSA (taken before taxes) |
| Roth 401(k) | Roth 401(k) or Roth 403(b) (taken after taxes) |
| Other deductions | Anything else, like union dues or after-tax insurance (taken after taxes) |
| Take-home (net) pay | What reached your bank |
| Notes (optional) | Anything you want to remember |

## Numbers must add up

Gross pay minus every tax and deduction must equal take-home pay, to the cent:

```
  $2,500.00  gross pay
-   $250.00  federal tax
-   $100.00  state tax
-   $155.00  Social Security
-    $36.25  Medicare
-   $150.00  401(k)
-    $50.00  HSA
-    $80.00  health insurance
-    $50.00  Roth 401(k)
-    $10.00  union dues
= $1,618.75  take-home pay
```

If the numbers don't add up, Budget Buddy tells you how far off you are. If your stub has a line that doesn't fit any field, add it to the closest one of the same kind (before tax or after tax) and mention it in the notes.

## Link the bank deposit (optional)

You can link a paycheck to the deposit in your checking or savings account. The deposit must be for exactly the take-home pay.

Your paycheck is never counted as income by itself. Income in your budget comes from your bank transactions, so linking a paycheck to its deposit just explains where that money came from. It is counted once, not twice.

## Year-to-date summary

Pick a year at the top to see totals for every paycheck that year: gross pay, each tax, each deduction, and take-home pay. The summary also totals taxes, before-tax deductions, and after-tax deductions.

### Social Security check

Budget Buddy checks whether Social Security tax looks right. Social Security tax is only charged on wages up to a yearly limit. The limit and rate come from your tax year's tax data file.

- **More than one job, too much in total:** each employer withholds on its own, so together they can take out more than the maximum. You can usually get the extra back as a credit on your federal tax return.
- **One employer took too much on its own:** that employer has to fix it. You can't claim it back on your tax return.

This check assumes every paycheck you entered is yours. If you also enter a spouse's paychecks, the check isn't meaningful yet.

## Something went wrong?

**"Please enter [field] as a number, like 12.50"**

Amounts must be numbers (for example 2500.50, not $2,500.50 or "twenty-five").

**"Net pay is [amount] more/less than gross pay minus all taxes and deductions"**

Your numbers don't add up. Check your pay stub and try again.

**"That deposit doesn't exist"**

The deposit you're trying to link isn't in your transactions. Make sure it's imported from your bank and has the exact take-home amount.
