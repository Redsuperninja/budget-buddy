# Tracking your charitable donations

> **Estimates, not tax advice.** Budget Buddy helps you keep the right records for your gifts to
> charity. It doesn't decide what you can deduct. Tax rules have many exceptions. Before you file,
> check IRS Publication 526, your tax software, or a tax professional.

Budget Buddy keeps a list of the gifts you give to charities and, for each tax year, tells you what proof to keep. Add gifts in the Budget Buddy dashboard on your computer: click **Taxes** at the top, then the **Donations** tab. You can also turn a payment you already have into a gift (see [Sort a payment into charity](#sort-a-payment-into-charity)).

## Logging a gift

For each gift, enter:

- **Date:** the day you paid. That's the day you mailed a check or the day your card was charged,
  even if you pay the card off later. For goods, it's the day you dropped them off.
- **Charity:** the charity's name.
- **Kind:** **cash** for money (cash, check, card, or bank transfer), or **goods** for things
  you gave, like clothes or furniture.
- **Amount:** for cash, how much you gave. For goods, what the items were worth when you gave them
  (what they'd sell for in their condition, often much less than you paid). If you got something
  back, like a dinner at a fundraiser, enter only the part above what it was worth.
- **Receipt (optional):** link a receipt you've already saved in Budget Buddy.
- **Notes (optional):** anything you want to remember. Notes are stored encrypted.

A few rules:

- The amount must be more than zero. If a gift was refunded or returned, edit or delete it instead.
- You can't log a gift for a future date. A promise to give (a pledge) counts once you pay it. A
  date one day ahead is allowed, in case your phone is in a time zone ahead of your computer.
- A gift counts in the calendar year of its date. A check mailed on December 31 counts in that
  year.

## Sort a payment into charity

If you gave by card, check or bank transfer, the payment is probably already in your transactions (typed in or
imported from your bank). You don't need to type it again:

1. Turn on the [All tools](open-the-dashboard.md#simple-view-or-all-tools) view, click **Money** at the top, then the **Transactions** tab, and find the payment.
2. Click **Mark as gift** in its row (the **Gift** column) and leave **Kind of gift** on **To a charity**.
3. Check the charity's name. Budget Buddy fills in what your bank called it, like "FAKE FOOD BANK"; change it
   to the name on the charity's receipt.
4. Check **Date you gave it**. It starts as the bank's date, but a gift counts on the day you mailed the check
   or your card was charged. Banks often show a later day (when a check cleared), which matters near the end
   of the year: a check mailed December 28 that cleared January 4 counts in December.
5. Check **Amount given**. It starts as the whole payment. If you got something back, like a dinner at a
   fundraiser gala, enter only the part above what that was worth. It can't be more than the payment.
6. Click **Save**. The row now says **Gift**, and the gift shows on the Donations tab as
   "Cash (from a payment)". If only part of the payment was a gift, the paperwork list asks you to keep the
   charity's statement of what you got back, since your bank record shows only the full payment.

A few things to know:

- Only money you paid out can become a gift. Money coming in, refunds, transfers between your own accounts and
  balance adjustments don't have a **Mark as gift** button.
- One payment can be one gift. Gifts of goods (clothes, furniture) aren't payments, so add them on the Donations
  tab instead.
- The payment moves to the **Charitable giving** category (a "wants" category Budget Buddy adds the first time).
  It still counts once as spending; the gift is your tax record, not a second expense. You can pick a
  different category for it afterwards. Give **Charitable giving** (and **Gifts**) a monthly budget on the
  Categories page, or let [the suggested budget](suggested-budget.md) pick one after a few months.
- While a payment is a gift, you can't change its amount or type, or link it as a transfer. To do that, remove
  the gift first (below).
- If you delete the payment, the gift stays in your donations.
- If the charity gives you money back (a cancelled event, a double charge), Budget Buddy doesn't change the gift
  for you. Lower or remove it on the Donations tab.

## Monthly or repeat giving

Once you've sorted one payment into charity (the whole payment as the gift), Budget Buddy recognizes the next
payment to the same charity in your bank file, or one you type in, and records it for you. It goes in **Charitable
giving** and on the **Donations** page as a cash gift for the whole payment, marked **Added automatically: a repeat
gift**. Check it like any other gift: the paperwork rules are the same (from $250, a letter from the charity).

- If it isn't a gift, click **Remove** on the Donations page. It won't be added back.
- A payment where only part was a gift (like a fundraiser dinner) is never repeated: enter those yourself.
- "The same charity" means the payment's description starts the same way, like `FAKE FOOD BANK`.

## A present for someone (a personal gift)

For a birthday or holiday present, pick **A personal gift** under **Kind of gift** and click **Save**. The
payment moves to the **Gifts** category so your budget shows what you spend on presents. Personal gifts aren't
tax deductible, so nothing is added to your donations. If you already have an income category called
**Gifts** (for presents you receive), Budget Buddy asks you to rename it first.

## Remove a gift

On the Donations tab, click **Remove** next to the gift and confirm. If it came from a payment, the payment stays
in your transactions and gets its **Mark as gift** button back. It stays in the **Charitable giving** category;
change it on the Transactions page if you like.

## What proof to keep

The yearly summary lists what to keep for each gift. The dollar amounts below are the ones for
2025 and 2026. Budget Buddy reads them from each year's tax data file, so they'll update if the IRS
changes them.

| Your gift | What to keep |
| --- | --- |
| Cash under $250 | A bank or card statement, or a receipt from the charity, showing the charity's name, the date, and the amount |
| Goods under $250 | A receipt from the charity with its name, the date, and a description of what you gave, plus your own note of what the items were worth |
| Any single gift of $250 or more (cash or goods) | A written acknowledgment from the charity, which you need before you file. It must show how much you gave (or describe the items) and whether you got anything in return, and if so, about what it was worth. A bank record alone isn't enough. |
| Goods of $250 or more | The written acknowledgment, plus your own note of what the items were worth and how you figured it |
| One goods gift worth more than $5,000 | Usually a qualified appraisal and Section B of Form 8283, signed by the charity. Some gifts, like publicly traded stock, don't need one. |

Separate gifts are not added together for the $250 rule. Two $150 gifts each need only a bank
record or receipt.

Some paperwork depends on all your gifts of goods for the year:

- **More than $500 of goods in total:** if you itemize, you'll likely need to file Form 8283 with
  your return. For each item, keep a note of when and how you got it and what you paid for it.
  Exactly $500 doesn't need it.
- **More than $5,000 of goods in total, but no single gift over $5,000:** if several were similar
  items (like clothing or furniture), add their values together. If those add up to more than
  $5,000, you'll likely need a qualified appraisal and Section B of Form 8283.

## Your yearly summary

The summary for a tax year shows:

- how many gifts you gave, and your cash, goods, and overall totals
- the proof to keep for each gift, and whether you've linked a receipt to it
- the paperwork that depends on the whole year (see above)
- the dollar thresholds used and the date that year's tax data was last checked

**These totals are what you gave, not what you can deduct.** How much counts depends on whether you
itemize and on your income. Budget Buddy doesn't work out your deduction yet.

### New for 2026: if you don't itemize

Starting with tax year 2026, people who take the standard deduction can still deduct some cash
gifts. The 2026 summary includes this note: "If you don't itemize, only cash gifts count, up to
$1,000.00 ($2,000.00 if married filing jointly)." Gifts of goods don't count for this. The 2025
summary doesn't show the note because the rule doesn't exist for 2025.

## Something went wrong?

| What you see | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| "Enter a gift on the day you actually gave it. A pledge counts once you pay it." | The date is in the future. Use the day you paid. |
| "A donation amount must be more than zero..." | Enter the amount you gave. To undo a refunded gift, edit or delete it. |
| "The amount can't be zero." | Enter the amount you gave, more than zero. |
| "That receipt doesn't exist. It may have been deleted." | Pick a receipt that's still saved, or leave the receipt empty. |
| "The gift can't be more than the payment it came from." | Enter only the part of the payment that was the gift. |
| "That payment is already recorded as a donation." | It's already on the Donations tab. Refresh the Transactions page. |
| "This payment is recorded as a donation. Remove the donation first..." | Remove the gift on the Donations tab, change the payment, then sort it into charity again. |
| "That donation doesn't exist. It may have been deleted." | The gift was already removed. Refresh your list. |
| "Budget Buddy doesn't have tax rules for [year] yet. Years it has: ..." | Summaries work only for years that have a tax data file. Your gifts are still saved, and the summary will work once that year is added. |
| "This only works on the computer where Budget Buddy is running." | Use the computer running Budget Buddy, or [connect this one](use-on-another-computer.md) first. |
